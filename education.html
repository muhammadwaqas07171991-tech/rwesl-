📚 Key Concepts Synthesized in this Educational Module

🔹 Spatial Rainfall Variation: Illustrates non-uniform precipitation intensity distribution across mountain basin topography.
🔹 Area-Weighted Rainfall Averaging: Delineates concentric isohyetal rings to calculate representative areal precipitation depth.
🔹 Moving Time-Window Analysis: Scans continuous storm hyetographs to identify peak precipitation epochs for critical durations.
🔹 Maximum Rainfall Depth Envelopes: Quantifies peak depth bounds across 2h, 4h, and 6h storm intervals.
🔹 Development and Interpretation of DAD Curves: Translates empirical storm physics into actionable design curves for hydraulic infrastructure sizing, dam safety, and AI-driven flood forecasting.

Theoretical Foundations

The 5 Hydrological Pillars of DAD Analysis

How Depth–Area–Duration relationships govern watershed precipitation behavior and empower predictive hydraulic engineering.

01

Spatial Rainfall Heterogeneity

Storm precipitation is never uniformly distributed across a natural drainage basin. The convective storm core exhibits intense localized precipitation rates (e.g., 140 mm/hr) which rapidly decrease with distance from the storm eye. DAD analysis models this attenuation curve precisely.

Topographic Forcing
02

Concentric Isohyetal Partitioning

To evaluate areal impacts, hydrologists draw isohyets—contours of equal rainfall depth. By segmenting the catchment into enclosed zones (Zone I, II, III), the spatial extent and volume of water generated across the landscape are systematically bounded.

Isohyetal Geometry
03

Automated Gauge Telemetry

Ground sensor arrays and digital tipping-bucket rain gauges stream high-frequency time-series observations. This continuous telemetric feedback validates remote sensing and radar estimates, providing ground truth for spot depth calculations.

IoT Sensor Networks
04

Area-Weighted Averaging

Point rain gauge measurements cannot be simply averaged arithmetically. Area-weighted depth integration computes the true representative rainfall volume contributing to watershed runoff across incremental footprint thresholds (100, 300, 600 km²).

Catchment Integration
05

Semi-Logarithmic Attenuation Curves

Plotting average rainfall depth against area on a semi-logarithmic scale yields smooth downward-sloping curves. As catchment area increases, maximum average depth naturally declines, establishing critical safety thresholds for flood defense design.

DAD Curve Formulation
06

Dam Safety & Spillway Design

Empirical DAD curves directly inform the calculation of Probable Maximum Precipitation (PMP) and design storm hydrographs, ensuring spillways, reservoirs, and flood walls in river basins like Namgang can safely route historic peak inflows.
