Active Project • Climate Change & Agriculture

Impact of Extreme Weather Events at Different Crop Growth Stages

Climate change is placing unprecedented pressure on agricultural productivity. Across the globe, extreme events such as heatwaves, droughts, flash floods, and cold snaps are intensifying. This research quantifies non-linear crop yield responses to critical temperature thresholds during sensitive physiological phases—such as flowering and grain-filling.

By linking systematic micro-climatic observation with agro-meteorological crop models, our lab establishes vulnerability baselines to guide climate-adaptive farming practices and decision-support systems.

Featured Scientific Initiative • Deep Learning & Hydrology

How Deep Learning Is Improving Evapotranspiration Estimates in South Korea

Potential evapotranspiration (PET) is fundamental to irrigation planning, drought assessment, and hydrological modeling. In heterogeneous catchments like the Nakdong River Basin (NRB), empirical formulas (Priestley–Taylor, Hargreaves–Samani) suffer from severe regional bias.

RWESL developed a hybrid CNN–Bidirectional LSTM with Attention Mechanism, leveraging 50+ years of meteorological records (1973–2024) across 13 ASOS stations. The hybrid model achieved exceptional precision (R² = 0.820, NSE = 0.820), consistently tracking daily cycles and seasonal evapotranspiration dynamics.

Active Project • Hydrogeology & Smart Agriculture

Seasonal Groundwater Trends & Water-Curtain Greenhouse Modeling in Gyeongnam

High-density greenhouse agriculture in Gyeongsangnam-Do heavily relies on water-curtain heating systems during winter. This intensive extraction leads to localized groundwater drawdown and recovery dynamics.

We develop hybrid statistical and recurrent deep learning frameworks to forecast seasonal groundwater depth, enabling sustainable aquifer management without compromising crop production.
